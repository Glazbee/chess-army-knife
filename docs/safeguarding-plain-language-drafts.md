# Safeguarding policy: plain-language wording (applied)

This wording was approved and is now in the plugin. It is kept here as a record of what changed.

The safeguarding example is a general version of a real club's policy, and it keeps that policy's wording. An approximate Flesch-Kincaid scan puts 43 of its sentences above grade 9, which is roughly the lower-secondary reading level that WCAG 2.2 AAA (3.1.5) asks for. The scan counts long words such as "safeguarding" heavily, so treat the grades as a guide.

These are shorter, plainer versions that keep each sentence's meaning. Because this is safeguarding wording, please check each one says what your club means, and ideally have your safeguarding officer read them. Where I have added something, it is marked.

Things worth knowing:

* Sentence 1 adds one line that was not in the original: "Safeguarding means keeping people safe from harm." AAA (3.1.3) asks for unusual words to be explained, and this is the main one.
* Where the original gave a long list of rules in one sentence, the draft splits it into short sentences.
* The proposed grades are the same rough estimate; a few stay above 9 because words like "safeguarding", "Disclosure and Barring Service" and "Federation" are unavoidable.


### 1. (grade 13.0 → 7.0)

**Now:** %s (the Club) is committed to ensuring everyone participating in chess does so in a safe, friendly, secure and enjoyable environment. Everyone at the Club, whether as a player, coach, official, administrator, staff member, volunteer, spectator, parent or carer, has a role to play. Individually and together, it is our actions, both on and off the chess board, that can help create a positive and inclusive culture.

**Proposed:** %s (the Club) wants everyone who plays chess to feel safe, welcome and able to enjoy it. Safeguarding means keeping people safe from harm. Everyone at the Club has a part to play: players, coaches, officials, administrators, staff, volunteers, spectators, parents and carers. What we do, on and off the chess board, helps build a friendly culture where everyone is included.

### 7. (grade 17.7 → 9.1)

**Now:** All participants in chess, whatever their age, gender, race, religion, sexual orientation, ability or disability, have the right to enjoy the game in an environment that is safe from abuse of any kind. The Club recognises that safeguarding starts with setting high standards and promoting a positive culture, which provides the best environment for participants to enjoy themselves and the game of chess.

**Proposed:** Everyone who plays chess has the right to enjoy the game and to be safe from abuse of any kind. This applies whatever their age, gender, race, religion, sexual orientation, ability or disability. Safeguarding starts with high standards and a positive culture. That gives everyone the best chance to enjoy themselves and the game.

### 8. (grade 13.0 → 6.5)

**Now:** We promote a listening culture, where the views of children, parents and carers, volunteers and other Club members are actively asked for and acted on. This helps us to create an environment where people have the chance and confidence to raise concerns, including concerns about poor practice, abuse and neglect.

**Proposed:** We listen. We ask children, parents and carers, volunteers and other members what they think, and we act on it. This helps people feel able to speak up about worries, including poor practice, abuse and neglect.

### 9. (grade 16.0 → 10.7)

**Now:** Ensure the safety of all children by providing effective supervision and proper planning of organised chess activities.

**Proposed:** Keep children safe by supervising them well and planning chess activities properly.

### 10. (grade 14.0 → 12.4)

**Now:** Consider the wellbeing and safety of participants before taking part in activities such as coaching or organising the playing of chess.

**Proposed:** Think about the wellbeing and safety of everyone taking part before you start an activity such as coaching or running a chess event.

### 11. (grade 13.3 → 15.2)

**Now:** Do not allow any discrimination on the grounds of religious beliefs, race, gender, social class or disability.

**Proposed:** Do not allow discrimination because of someone's religion, race, gender, social class or disability.

### 12. (grade 13.5 → 13.5)

**Now:** Report accidents or incidents of alleged abuse or poor practice to the Safeguarding Officer.

**Proposed:** Tell the Safeguarding Officer about any accident, or any suspected abuse or poor practice.

### 13. (grade 10.5 → 8.6)

**Now:** Do not accept or give individual gifts to children and young people without permission from their parents or guardians.

**Proposed:** Do not give or accept gifts to or from children and young people without their parents' or guardians' permission.

### 14. (grade 11.1 → 8.3)

**Now:** Do not add minors to your social media accounts or take their telephone numbers unless their parents have given permission.

**Proposed:** Do not add children to your social media accounts, or take their phone numbers, unless their parents have said you may.

### 15. (grade 18.8 → 8.1)

**Now:** Never take children to your home, hotel bedroom or similar place (for example for coaching) unless another person is there who is, or is authorised by, their parent or guardian, or you have the explicit consent of their parent or guardian.

**Proposed:** Never take children to your home, a hotel bedroom or a similar place (for example for coaching) unless one of these is true. Another person is there who is the child's parent or guardian, or who the parent or guardian has approved. Or the parent or guardian has clearly agreed.

### 16. (grade 9.8 → 7.1)

**Now:** Plan activities so that more than one other person is present, or at least so that they are within sight or hearing of others where possible. This applies to activities such as one-to-one training and travelling to or from chess events.

**Proposed:** Plan activities so that at least one other person is there, or so that others can see or hear what is going on. This matters most for one-to-one training and for travelling to or from chess events.

**Now:** All social media accounts will be password-protected, and at least two members of staff or volunteers will have access to each account and password.

**Proposed:** Every social media account will have a password. At least two members of staff or volunteers will have access to each account and its password.

### 19. (grade 12.9 → 3.0)

**Now:** The accounts will be monitored by at least two designated Club members, appointed by the Club's committee, to provide transparency.

**Proposed:** At least two Club members, chosen by the committee, will watch each account. This keeps things open.

### 20. (grade 16.7 → 10.4)

**Now:** The designated members managing our online presence will ask the Safeguarding Officer for advice on safeguarding requirements.

**Proposed:** The members who run our online presence will ask the Safeguarding Officer for advice on keeping people safe.

### 21. (grade 15.6 → 8.2)

**Now:** Designated members will remove inappropriate posts by children or adults, explaining why, and tell anyone who may be affected (as well as the parents of any children involved).

**Proposed:** These members will remove inappropriate posts by children or adults and say why. They will tell anyone affected, and the parents of any children involved.

### 22. (grade 13.5 → 5.3)

**Now:** The Club will make sure children know who manages our social media accounts and who to contact if they have any concerns about something that has happened online.

**Proposed:** The Club will make sure children know who runs our social media accounts. They will also know who to tell if something online worries them.

### 23. (grade 9.5 → 9.2)

**Now:** Our account, page and event settings will be set to "private" so that only invited Club members can see their content.

**Proposed:** Our account, page and event settings will be private, so only invited Club members can see what is posted.

### 24. (grade 11.1 → 9.1)

**Now:** Identifying details such as a child's home address, school name or telephone number should not be posted on social media platforms.

**Proposed:** Do not post details that could identify a child on social media, such as a home address, school name or phone number.

### 25. (grade 17.0 → 10.8)

**Now:** Parents will be asked to give their approval for Club members to communicate with their children through Club social media accounts, video conferencing platforms or any other means of communication.

**Proposed:** We will ask parents to agree to Club members contacting their children through Club social media accounts, video calls or any other way.

### 26. (grade 9.2 → 9.1)

**Now:** Parents will need to give permission for photographs or videos of their child to be posted on social media.

**Proposed:** Parents must give permission before photos or videos of their child are posted on social media.

### 28. (grade 11.2 → 7.0)

**Now:** Club members will avoid having children's personal mobile numbers and will instead make contact through a parent.

**Proposed:** Club members will avoid having children's own mobile numbers. They will contact a parent instead.

### 29. (grade 14.1 → 10.0)

**Now:** A method of accountability will be arranged, such as copies of texts, messages or emails also being sent to another member of staff or to parents.

**Proposed:** There will be a way to check what is said, such as copying texts, messages or emails to another member of staff or to a parent.

### 30. (grade 12.7 → 8.4)

**Now:** Smartphone users should respect the private lives of others and not take or share pictures or videos of other people if it could invade their privacy.

**Proposed:** Do not take or share pictures or videos of other people if it could invade their privacy.

### 31. (grade 10.5 → 7.1)

**Now:** Texts, emails or messages will be used for passing on information, such as details of upcoming chess matches. A group chat may sometimes have casual conversation, but always in a good spirit. No offensive communications will be tolerated.

**Proposed:** We will use texts, emails and messages to pass on information, such as details of chess matches. A group chat may sometimes be casual, but it must always be friendly. We will not accept offensive messages.

### 32. (grade 14.8 → 6.7)

**Now:** Club members should not "friend" or "follow" children from personal accounts on social media, and should keep the same professional boundaries online as they would in person when using Club accounts.

**Proposed:** Club members should not "friend" or "follow" children from their personal accounts. When using Club accounts, they should keep the same boundaries online as they would in person.

### 33. (grade 13.0 → 12.3)

**Now:** Club members should make sure any content they post on public personal accounts is accurate and appropriate, as children may "follow" them on social media.

**Proposed:** Club members should make sure anything they post on public personal accounts is accurate and suitable, because children may "follow" them.

### 34. (grade 17.6 → 4.8)

**Now:** Rather than communicating with parents through personal social media accounts, Club members should choose a more formal means of communication, such as face to face, in an email or in writing, or use a Club account or website.

**Proposed:** Club members should not contact parents through personal social media. They should use a more formal way: face to face, an email or a letter, or a Club account or website.

### 35. (grade 13.4 → 12.4)

**Now:** Club members should avoid communicating with children by email or Club social media such as messaging apps without copying in parents or guardians.

**Proposed:** Club members should not email or message children, including through Club social media or messaging apps, without copying in a parent or guardian.

### 36. (grade 15.7 → 7.9)

**Now:** Emails or messages should keep the Club's tone and be written in a professional manner, in the same way you would write to fellow professionals, avoiding kisses (X's), slang or inappropriate language.

**Proposed:** Emails and messages should be polite and professional, as if writing to a colleague. Do not use kisses (X's), slang or inappropriate language.

### 37. (grade 9.9 → 5.8)

**Now:** Club members should not delete any messages or communications sent to or from Club accounts.

**Proposed:** Club members should not delete messages sent to or from Club accounts.

### 38. (grade 9.3 → 5.9)

**Now:** Our aim is that everyone at the Club should feel confident to raise a concern, no matter how small. We believe that raising and dealing with concerns quickly, when they happen, supports a proactive safeguarding culture at the Club.

**Proposed:** We want everyone at the Club to feel able to raise a concern, however small. Dealing with concerns quickly, as they come up, helps us keep people safe.

### 39. (grade 12.1 → 7.9)

**Now:** All suspicions, concerns and allegations will be taken seriously. We will follow the 3 Rs with every concern: responding appropriately, recording confidentially and reporting where necessary, so that concerns are dealt with fairly and promptly.

**Proposed:** We will take every worry, suspicion and allegation seriously. For every concern we follow the 3 Rs: respond properly, record it in confidence, and report it where needed. This way concerns are dealt with fairly and quickly.

### 40. (grade 15.8 → 6.7)

**Now:** The Club recognises that it is not the responsibility of Club members to decide or investigate whether abuse has taken place, but to act on and report any concerns promptly.

**Proposed:** Club members do not have to decide whether abuse has happened, or investigate it. Their job is to act on a concern and report it quickly.

### 41. (grade 12.3 → 8.6)

**Now:** We make sure that confidential information about safeguarding is shared appropriately and only with those who need to know. Information may need to be shared with the ECF Safeguarding Officer or with local agencies that have statutory responsibility for safeguarding. If we are unsure, we will ask the ECF for advice.

**Proposed:** We share private information about safeguarding only with people who need to know. We may need to share it with the ECF Safeguarding Officer, or with local agencies that are responsible for safeguarding by law. If we are not sure, we will ask the ECF for advice.
